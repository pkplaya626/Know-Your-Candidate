About & sources

Whose site this is, where the facts on it come from, how often they are read again, and how to tell us when something is wrong.

An independent project

Know Your Candidate is an independent, non-partisan project. Its code and data are public, at https://github.com/pkplaya626/Know-Your-Candidate, so anyone can see how each fact was reached, and every change to them is recorded there.

It is not affiliated with any campaign, party, candidate or government office, takes no money from any of them, and carries no advertising. It is served by GitHub Pages.

What this site is not

It is not an official election source, and nothing on it is legal advice. Before you register, request a ballot or vote, check with your state's election office: each state's page here links its official voting pages, and NASS Can I Vote lists every state's.

Report a problem

This site makes claims about real, named people, and a wrong one matters. If something is wrong, out of date or missing, or a page does not work for you, open a report on GitHub (it needs a free GitHub account). Every profile and page has the same link, which fills in where you were. Please say what is wrong and, if you can, where the right answer is published.

Reports and the corrections made from them are public: each is an issue in the repository, and each fix a change anyone can read.

Where the data comes from

Most facts are read from a named source by a script and checked before they are published. The member rosters' biographies, and the decisions no source can settle alone, are kept by hand in the repository, where each change is public. Where a source says nothing, the site says so, rather than guessing. The repository's README lists every field and where it comes from.

The scheduled refresh runs every day from Oct. 15 to Nov. 2, 2026 and from Nov. 4 to Dec. 15, 2026, the weeks before the election and the six weeks after it, and from Jan. 3 to Jan. 14, 2027, as the new Congress is sworn in. On other days it runs on Mondays and Thursdays before Election Day and on Mondays after it. Each run is published only once every check passes. Market prices and polling averages are read every 12 hours, at 00:47 and 12:47 UTC, and stop when the first polls close on Election Day: prices that move on early returns would read as results.

Who counts as a candidate: everyone the FEC records as having raised or spent more than $5,000 for the 2026 cycle (52 U.S.C. §30101(2)). Everyone who has registered is counted in each race, whatever they raised.

Privacy

  • No cookies, no analytics, no advertising trackers and no accounts. The site does not know who you are.
  • Your browser keeps two settings for this site, on your device only: the theme you picked and the last state you looked at, so the navigation can offer it again. To clear them, clear this site's data in your browser's settings.
  • Portraits and some images load from the sites that publish them: Wikimedia Commons, the House Clerk, Congress.gov, state legislatures and campaign sites. Those sites see your IP address when your browser fetches an image, as any website would; this site asks your browser not to tell them which page you were reading.
  • “Find my district” asks your browser for your location and places it on the map on your device. It is never sent anywhere.
  • The site is served by GitHub Pages, which receives your IP address with each request, as any web host does; see GitHub’s privacy statement. Links out lead to other sites, whose own policies apply.

Accessibility

The site aims to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA. Every page is built and checked by automated tests on each change: pages can be zoomed; every control works from a keyboard, with a visible focus; dialogs hold the focus and return it when they close; each page has a link to skip to its content; fields are large enough on a phone that it does not zoom on its own; and the words on the maps are page text, so they stay readable in forced dark modes.

Known limits: the maps are visual. On a map of the states, each state is a button that names it, and picking one shows its facts as text, but no view lists every state's value at once. Documents this site links, such as disclosure PDFs and the Senate's disclosure site, are their publishers' and may not be accessible. If anything here is hard to use, tell us with the same form.
