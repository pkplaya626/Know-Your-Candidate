Statewide questions on the Nov 3, 2026 ballot

10 statewide questions are on the Nov 3, 2026 ballot, as the Louisiana Secretary of State lists them: 2026 Proposed Constitutional Amendments, November 3, 2026 Election (Louisiana Secretary of State), read Oct. 19, 2026.

  1. Proposed Amendment No. 1: Allows a one-time transfer of a property tax exemption for the surviving spouse of a disabled veteran (source)
  2. Proposed Amendment No. 2: Authorizes a local taxing authority to levy a lower millage rate without losing its ability to adjust to the prior year's maximum authorized millage rate (source)
  3. Proposed Amendment No. 3: Prohibits post-conviction bail for a defendant who is convicted of an aggravated offense against a victim who is a minor (source)
  4. Proposed Amendment No. 4: Places a lifetime term limit on a person who has served as governor for more than one and one-half terms (source)
  5. Proposed Amendment No. 5: Authorizes a state retirement system receiving nonrecurring state monies to apply those monies to any of its outstanding unfunded accrued liability (UAL) rather than its oldest UAL first (source)
  6. Proposed Amendment No. 6: Authorizes extension of an additional property tax exemption for owners who are 65 or older (source)
  7. Proposed Amendment No. 7: Authorizes the use of public funds to identify, inventory, remove, or replace certain water lines on property owned by utility customers (source)
  8. Proposed Amendment No. 8: Prohibits expropriation of property by a foreign adversary or an agent of a foreign adversary (source)
  9. Proposed Amendment No. 9: Increases the income limit for qualifying for the special property tax assessment level from $100,000, to be adjusted annually for inflation beginning in 2026, to $150,000, to be adjusted annually for inflation beginning in 2028 (source)
  10. Proposed Amendment No. 10: Authorizes an exemption from property taxes for blighted or derelict property that has been rehabilitated and requires the legislature to enact laws relative to implementation and administration of the exemption (source)

Each is linked to the page its official text is on. This site does not say what a yes or a no vote does.

Local questions - a county's, a city's or a school district's - are not listed here.
